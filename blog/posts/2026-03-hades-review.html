Hades - The Perfect Busy Gamer Game

Back to Blog

Game Information

Platform PC, Switch, PS4/5, Xbox
Genre Action Roguelike
Developer Supergiant Games
Release Date September 2020
Time per Session 20-45 minutes
Time to Beat 25-30 hours (main), 100+ (completionist)

Busy Gamer Rating

★★★★★

5/5 Stars - Absolutely perfect for limited gaming time. This is THE game to play when you only have 30 minutes.

The Pitch

You play as Zagreus, son of Hades, attempting to escape the underworld. Each escape attempt (called a "run") takes between 20-45 minutes depending on your skill and luck. When you die (and you will die a lot), you return to the House of Hades to try again, but with new abilities, weapons, and story progression.

Why It's Perfect for Busy Gamers

Hades is a masterclass in respecting your time. Here's why it's absolutely perfect for players with limited gaming hours:

Fixed Session Length: Each run has a natural endpoint. You either escape (20-45 minutes) or you die trying. There's no awkward "I need to find a save point" moment. You can commit to one run and be done, guilt-free.

Meaningful Progression Every Time: Even when you die (especially when you die), you're making progress. You earn darkness, gemstones, diamonds, and other resources that unlock permanent upgrades. You advance character relationships through excellent dialogue. You unlock new weapons and abilities. Death isn't failure—it's part of the story.

No FOMO: Unlike live-service games or multiplayer titles, Hades will wait patiently for you. Take a week off? A month? The game picks up exactly where you left it, and you won't have missed any events or fallen behind your friends.

Easy to Jump In: The gameplay loop is simple to understand but deep enough to master over time. You're always doing the same thing (escaping), but each run feels fresh thanks to different weapon choices, god boons, and enemy combinations.

The Gameplay Loop

Combat is fast-paced, responsive, and incredibly satisfying. You have a basic attack, special attack, dash, and cast ability. Simple enough to learn in minutes, but with enough depth to keep you engaged for dozens of hours.

The roguelike structure means every run is different. You'll choose from random power-ups (boons) offered by various Greek gods, creating unique builds each time. One run you might focus on lightning damage from Zeus, the next on poison effects from Dionysus. This variety keeps the game fresh even after many hours.

How It Respects Your Schedule

This is crucial for busy gamers: Hades never wastes your time. There's no grinding for the sake of grinding. No fetch quests. No unskippable cutscenes on subsequent viewings. Every minute you play advances either your skill, your character power, or the story.

The game also has an excellent "God Mode" option for accessibility. Turn it on and you gain 2% damage reduction every time you die, up to 80%. This isn't cheating—it's a way for players with less time to practice to still experience the full story without frustration.

Pros for Busy Gamers

  • Perfect 20-45 minute session length
  • Meaningful progress even when you fail
  • No grinding or time-wasting mechanics
  • Easy to pick up after days/weeks away
  • Incredible story that unfolds gradually
  • Multiple difficulty options including God Mode
  • Works great on Switch for portable play
  • High replay value without feeling repetitive

Cons for Busy Gamers

  • "Just one more run" syndrome is real—hard to stop playing
  • Early runs can feel slow before you unlock more content
  • Completionist content requires 100+ hours
  • Some story elements require many runs to unlock
  • Can be challenging if you're rusty with action games

Final Verdict

If you're a busy gamer looking for something that respects your limited time while delivering incredible gameplay, story, and value, Hades is an absolute must-play. It's become my go-to recommendation for friends who say "I don't have time for games anymore."

Buy this game. You won't regret it.

Time Investment: 25-30 hours for main story | 50-70 hours for true ending | 100+ hours for completionist

Best For: Anyone with 30 minutes at a time | Fans of action combat | Story enthusiasts

Skip If: You hate dying repeatedly (even with progression) | You need deep RPG systems

Elden Ring - Worth the Time Investment?

Back to Blog

Game Information

Platform PC, PS4/5, Xbox
Genre Action RPG, Souls-like
Developer FromSoftware
Release Date February 2022
Time per Session 1-3 hours (recommended)
Time to Beat 80-120 hours (main), 150+ (completionist)

Busy Gamer Rating

★★★☆☆

3/5 Stars - A masterpiece, but demands significant time investment. Best for weekend warriors or those who can commit to long-term gaming projects.

The Pitch

Elden Ring is FromSoftware's take on the open-world formula, combining Dark Souls' challenging combat with the freedom to explore a massive, interconnected fantasy world. You're a Tarnished, seeking to become the Elden Lord by collecting Great Runes and defeating demigods in a shattered realm.

The Busy Gamer Reality Check

Let me be upfront: Elden Ring is not designed for quick gaming sessions. While you technically can play for 30-45 minutes at a time, the game really shines when you can sink in for 2-3 hour sessions. Here's why:

Exploration Takes Time: The open world is massive and filled with secrets. Riding from one location to another, exploring caves, finding items, and discovering new areas all adds up. You'll often think "I'll just check what's over that hill" and suddenly an hour has passed.

Boss Fights Are Commitments: Major boss fights can take 30-60 minutes of attempts (or more) before you succeed. The game doesn't have pause (except in offline mode), and leaving mid-boss means starting over. You need focused, uninterrupted time.

Progress Can Feel Slow: With only an hour or two per week, you might spend entire sessions stuck on a single boss or area. This can feel frustrating when you're already time-limited.

But If You Have the Time...

Here's the thing: Elden Ring is one of the best games ever made. If you can carve out 5-10 hours per week, or dedicate weekends to gaming, this is absolutely worth your time. The sense of exploration, discovery, and accomplishment is unmatched.

The open-world structure actually helps busy gamers in one key way: you have freedom in how you tackle challenges. Stuck on a boss? Go explore somewhere else. Every area you explore makes you stronger, giving you more options when you return.

Unlike linear games where you must beat that one boss to progress, Elden Ring lets you create your own path. Hit a wall? There are dozens of other directions to explore, each with their own rewards and challenges.

Tips for Time-Strapped Players

If you decide to take on Elden Ring with limited time, here are strategies that helped me:

1. Use Guides Strategically: Don't be ashamed to look up where to find items or how to beat a specific boss. Your time is valuable—don't waste it being lost or frustrated.

2. Focus on Your Build: Pick a build (melee, magic, faith) early and stick with it. Spreading your stats too thin will make the game much harder and waste your limited playtime.

3. Summon Help: Use spirit ashes or online co-op. This isn't "cheating"—it's using the game's built-in systems to respect your time.

4. Set Session Goals: "Today I'll explore Limgrave's caves" or "Today I'll attempt Margit." Having a goal prevents aimless wandering and makes sessions feel productive.

5. Play Offline to Pause: In offline mode, you can pause the game by opening the menu and going to "Menu Explanation." This lets you handle real-life interruptions.

The Honest Assessment

Elden Ring is a 80-120 hour commitment minimum. At 2 hours per week, that's nearly a year of gaming. At 5 hours per week, it's still 4-5 months. This isn't a quick experience—it's a gaming marathon.

The game is incredible, but you need to ask yourself: Do you want one amazing long-term experience, or would you rather play multiple shorter games in that time? There's no wrong answer, but be honest with yourself about your gaming goals and available time.

Pros for Busy Gamers

  • Open world lets you tackle challenges in any order
  • Can walk away from difficult content and return later
  • Tons of build variety for different playstyles
  • Co-op and spirit summons can speed up progress
  • Incredible world design rewards exploration
  • No missable content on first playthrough (mostly)
  • Can pause in offline mode

Cons for Busy Gamers

  • 80-120+ hours minimum for completion
  • Boss fights require extended uninterrupted time
  • Easy to forget controls/strategies between sessions
  • Steep learning curve takes time to master
  • Can feel directionless with limited playtime
  • No quest log or journal to remember objectives
  • Exploration sessions can easily run long
  • Progress feels slow with only 1-2 hours per week

Final Verdict

Elden Ring is a masterpiece that demands respect—and time. If you have 5-10 hours per week or can dedicate weekends to gaming, absolutely play this. It's one of the best games ever made.

If you only have 1-2 hours per week, consider whether you want to commit 6-12 months to a single game. The experience is incredible, but there are better options for extremely limited gaming time.

Play it if you have the time. Skip it if you don't. But don't skip it forever.

Time Investment: 80-120 hours for main story | 150+ hours for completionist

Best For: Weekend warriors | Fans of challenging combat | Exploration enthusiasts

Skip If: You only have 1-2 hours per week | You get frustrated easily | You need clear objectives

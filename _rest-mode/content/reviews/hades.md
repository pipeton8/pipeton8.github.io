## The pitch

You play as Zagreus, son of Hades, attempting to escape the underworld. Each escape attempt (called a "run") takes between 20 and 45 minutes depending on your skill and luck. When you die (and you will die a lot), you return to the House of Hades to try again, but with new abilities, weapons, and story progression.

## Why it works in short sessions

**Fixed session length.** Each run has a natural endpoint. You either escape or you die trying. There's no awkward "I need to find a save point" moment. You can commit to one run and be done, guilt-free.

**Meaningful progression every time.** Even when you die, you're making progress: resources that unlock permanent upgrades, character relationships, new weapons and abilities. Death isn't failure, it's part of the story.

**No FOMO.** Hades will wait patiently for you. Take a week off? A month? The game picks up exactly where you left it.

## The gameplay loop

Combat is fast-paced, responsive, and incredibly satisfying. You have a basic attack, special attack, dash, and cast ability. Simple enough to learn in minutes, but with enough depth to keep you engaged for dozens of hours.

## Pros

- Natural 20–45 minute session length
- Meaningful progress even when you fail
- Easy to pick up after days or weeks away
- God Mode makes the story reachable on a tight schedule

## Cons

- "Just one more run" is real: hard to stop playing
- Completionist content requires 100+ hours

## Verdict

If you want something that respects your limited time while delivering incredible gameplay, story, and value, Hades is an absolute must-play. It's become the go-to recommendation for friends who say "I don't have time for games anymore."

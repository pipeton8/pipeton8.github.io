## What is “rest mode”?

rest mode is a video game review blog for people whose free time is too fragmented or short to spend it on any random game. Reviews on the site have the main purpose of letting players know how a game feels if you are forced to play in short stretches or for only a few hours a week. Is the story less enjoyable because you spent a whole week without playing? Is this a roguelike with 1hr runs that you can’t complete on each of the 45min breaks you get while your kid is sleeping? These are the types of questions that reviews will be tailored to answer, directly from firsthand experience.

But that’s not all! I like writing and I love games as an art form, so I also want the reviews to convey how the game makes you _feel_. A review based on time use will be incomplete if the game is not as interesting, especially if you are short on time! This means that reviews will also speak about the traditional aspects of a game: pitch, gameplay elements, story (with as few spoilers as possible!), and others.

Perhaps one of the most interesting quirks of the underlying theme of the blog is that, by virtue of the time constraints I face, many reviews will arrive “late”. It is very likely that many reviewers have already made their assessments by the time I feel ready to write something about a game. If anything, I see this as a feature and not a bug. Hopefully, I can bring a fresh take on a game that has been talked about extensively, or help people revisit the feeling of a game they finished a while ago. Or maybe I help you choose your next target in your backlog! Regardless of who you are (a busy person like me or someone just looking to read game commentary) I hope you find something valuable in each of the reviews I prepare for you.

## Who’s behind the site?

I am [Felipe del Canto](https://pipeton8.github.io), an economics PhD student, husband and dad. After our kid was born, gaming hours had to change drastically. Nap times are short and we want our kid to be as screen free as possible. This meant that we had either an hour or so to play during the day and a few before we went to sleep (we can’t go to sleep too late, mind you; our little alarm clock likes to start the day early). That is when the idea of a blog like this one started. However, it would take a while to actually start.

My gaming life has been long and I think I have played quite a long list of games (at least 438 I have in [my Backloggd](https://bckl.gg/u9Mi))! This is one of my most important hobbies and one of my most enjoyable ways to spend my free time. I also like a wide variety of genres and styles, and I am always open to new games outside of my comfort zone. However, software is not the only part of this world that I enjoy. Gaming hardware is actually one of the gaming aspects I like the most. Opening a new console, TV/monitor, or PC is one of my most enjoyable activities (not that I can do that too often!). Luckily, I’ve managed to gather a few gaming devices I plan to use for the reviews.

My main device is a PS5. I’ve had a PlayStation since 2010 when I bought the PS3 and haven’t changed since. I like the XBOX though! The design is great (especially the Series X and S) but I have invested too much time in my trophies to move to a different ecosystem (120 platinums as of July 2026!). My second device for gaming is a Lenovo Legion Go. I went for a handheld in 2024 trying to get some access to PC gaming and emulation without spending thousands of dollars on a gaming PC/laptop. The Legion Go’s big screen and somewhat affordable price convinced me and I can’t say I regret it. Of course, the Steam Deck has many better features but having native access to alternative stores and Game Pass was a priority. My third and final device is a Switch OLED. I got an OG Switch in 2019 and updated to the OLED in 2022 (kudos to my wife for the birthday present!). This device’s main job is to play Nintendo exclusive titles, and for that I mostly mean Pokémon games. I also played the open world Zelda games (BOTW and TOTK) and even the chibi-inspired ones (Link’s Awakening and Echoes of Wisdom). I also used it somewhat sparingly to play some multiplatform titles like Hollow Knight and Death’s Door, but that has been the exception rather than the rule. I’ll try to include comments about performance on the platform I used but given my PC setup do not expect deep comments about ray/path tracing and 4K visuals running at 120FPS.

## What made me start this blog?

As I said before, the idea for a blog like this one was born around mid-2025, when our kid was born. However, that would remain an idea until May 2026, when I started building a prototype of the site and reading/hearing many reviews I liked so I could find some style I wanted to base mine on. I also started taking notes about the games I was currently playing in order to collect my thoughts later in a review. Even then, this blog wouldn’t see the light of day for a few months. In the end, two things moved me to put some serious effort into launching it: one announcement and an old game.

The main nudge was Sony’s announcement on July 1st (2026) that they would be [phasing out discs in 2028](https://blog.playstation.com/2026/07/01/physical-disc-production-ending-in-january-2028-for-new-games-releasing-on-playstation-consoles/). I’ve been around enough to have used plenty of physical versions of games. For my PS3 and for a big part of the lifetime of my PS4 I even had a binder full of games. Whether you experience gaming like this or not, it is clear that a world where games are only available digitally looks quite different from what we know (even if for PC this has been the norm for a long time!). I am not extremely concerned about delistings or “personalized pricing”, I think those are either unlikely at scale or easily avoidable through regulation. However, I do feel that _something_ will change. It might be that the composition of players within console ecosystems shifts. Or maybe many people will be discouraged from gaming at all: potential gamers might find cheaper hobbies or alternatives (mobile, perhaps?). Maybe the sense of community will be reduced, as “sharing games” is for now impossible in digital format (at least on console in a “simple” way). All of that will eventually change what games get made, and I do worry that the people that contributed the most to the _art_ component of the medium will use their talents elsewhere. For someone that loves this culture, the prospect of losing such an amount of talent is sad, to say the least. I thought that if this is a time of major change for an industry I am so invested in, I might as well start writing about it.

Around the same time as this announcement, my wife and I finished playing _What Remains of Edith Finch_ (as I said, some reviews might take time to arrive!). PS+ gave this game for free in May 2019 and I hadn’t played it since. I hope the review conveys how impressive this game is, because it represents one of the best attempts I’ve seen in gaming to create a truly _artistic_ experience: to share a strong message through art and player interaction. This game made us feel so. many. things. that I needed to put them into words. Besides, this is one of the perfect games for rest mode: the full game is short, each of the game’s stories is short, and while the story is emotionally complex, it is not difficult nor does it require the player to memorize too many elements. In a rush, I would say that this game is _the_ symbol for this blog. To clarify, this is not saying that this game is a masterpiece (which I might be tempted to agree with) or that I haven’t played/will play something better. In the end, my message is that one of the main pillars of this blog is what games can make players _feel_. It is not about games being good or bad, but about the craftsmanship that comes with this art form, and how it can be used (or misused!) to produce something in their audience. I hope I can translate some of those feelings in my reviews.

Hope you enjoy your visit!

Felipe
